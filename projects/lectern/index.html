Meet Lectern.

Organise every subject, turn lecture slides into great notes automatically, write your own, and study with flashcards, quizzes and a study coach. Summaries are checked statement by statement against the slides they cite, and everything is saved as normal Markdown files in a folder you choose.

Features

Lectern

  1. 01 / 05

    Slides in. Great notes out.

    Drop in a PDF, PowerPoint or Google Slides link. Lectern reads every slide and writes structured notes with clickable slide citations, key terms and likely exam questions.

    17 summary formatsFrom Lectern's README

    Screenshot: A summary that follows the source PDF's own headings

  2. 02 / 05

    Summaries you can check.

    Lectern pulls out the facts first, writes from them, then checks every statement against the slide it cites. Anything it can't confirm is flagged.

    Screenshot: Summary Studio with format, length and focus options beside a live preview and a verification report

  3. 03 / 05

    Flashcards that cover everything.

    Cards made from the checked facts on every slide, linked back to their source and scheduled with FSRS, the modern spaced-repetition algorithm.

    Screenshot: Generated flashcards grouped by slide, with accept and reject buttons

  4. 04 / 05

    Study Packs in one click.

    A summary, flashcards, a practice quiz and a cheat sheet for any lecture, ready before your tutorial.

    A planner built for semesters.

    Week numbers, your timetable, assessments and "Plan my week", with an "I'm behind" catch-up for the weeks that get away from you.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

  1. Lectern.dmg

    1. Open the download

    Double-click Lectern.dmg in your Downloads folder.

  2. Applications

    2. Drag it to Applications

    Drag Lectern onto the Applications folder.

    • Open
    • Show in Finder
    • Get Info

    3. Right-click → Open, the first time

    Then click Open again. After that, open it normally.

Why the extra step? I'm a student, so my apps aren't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). macOS asks you to confirm once that you trust an app from the internet. The app is exactly the one built from my source, and you can check the checksum below.

What's new

Version 1.9.0

23 November 2026

Lecture notes in Summary Studio, Process Lectures, smarter default models and automatic search by meaning

Changed

  • Generate Notes is now Summary Studio's "Lecture notes" type: overview, objectives, detailed notes by topic with slide citations and practice questions, key terms (into the subject glossary) and a summary, built from the same checked facts as every summary and checked against the slides. Notes from a link use it too.
  • Process Lectures… (Study menu, and a subject or folder's right-click menu) makes lecture notes, summaries, flashcards or Study Packs for every lecture in one place, replacing Summarise All…, Make Study Packs, Generate Flashcards for All Lectures and the Processing Queue menu item.
  • Auto is the default model for each task: your chosen model writes notes, summaries and chat; a small, fast model finds and checks facts and makes flashcards and quizzes (Haiku if you use Claude). Choices you made yourself are kept.
  • Search by meaning turns on by itself when an embedding model (such as embeddinggemma) is installed in Ollama; you can still turn it off.

Install: download Lectern.dmg, drag Lectern to Applications, then right-click it and choose Open the first time (the app isn't notarized).

  • v1.8.023 Nov 2026Notes

    Complete quality pass: faster, more reliable, easier to use, and a full Feature Guide

    The "1.4.0" quality release (numbered 1.8.0 so it can update 1.7.0): a complete audit of every feature, with the fixes it led to. See AUDIT.md, FEATURES.md and BUGS.md in the source repository.

    Added

    • Update Now in Settings → Updates: what's new, then download, check and install in one click, with progress and plain-English errors (Download Manually, Copy error details).
    • Help → Feature Guide: everything Lectern does, searchable, with Show Me buttons. Also as a PDF (Lectern User Guide).
    • Help → Troubleshoot…: rebuild the search index or embeddings, clear AI caches, check Ollama and models, reset the processing queue, reconnect the University folder, restore a backup, and Safe Mode (also by holding Option while Lectern opens).
    • Report a Problem… saves a diagnostics file to your Desktop (versions, settings, recent log, never your notes) for you to read before attaching it to the GitHub issue it opens.
    • Diagnostics (Settings, with advanced settings shown): index, queue, caches with Clear, AI use, Ollama status and the recent log; optional local-only counts of which features you use (off by default, never sent anywhere).
    • Settings search, a description of each section, Restore Defaults per section, and "Show advanced settings".
    • Keyboard Shortcuts is searchable and lists every shortcut.

    Changed

    • Settings is a sidebar instead of ten tabs (Backup and Updates were hidden behind an overflow arrow).
    • Every AI action shows the same progress (with time left and Cancel) and the same error card with the way forward.
    • Background work pauses while the Mac sleeps, when Ollama stops, when the University folder goes missing or the disk is nearly full, and resumes afterwards; AI jobs with no progress for 10 minutes are stopped with Resume.
    • Summary Studio, the flashcard panel, the Planner, Search and subject pages adapt to small windows.
    • Reduce Motion and Increase Contrast are respected throughout, including in the editor.
    • Text alignment is ⌃⌘L/E/R/J and subscript ⇧⌘, (the old shortcuts were taken by menu commands and never worked).

    Fixed

    • Text typed just before renaming or moving the open page was lost, and deleting the open page could bring it back.
    • Typing straight after creating a page could be thrown away.
    • An AI result put into the open page (a queued summary, a Study Pack, Insert in Summary Studio, flashcards, a planner tick) could overwrite what you'd typed in the last moment; switching pages straight after typing could also drop the last few words.
    • A page changed in another app or on another device while open in Lectern could be overwritten by the next autosave; both versions are now kept.
    • Changes made outside Lectern were ignored for University folders reached through a symlink, and pages there could get the wrong path.
    • Summaries of long lectures spend about a quarter of the time on Lectern's own processing (fact de-duplication was comparing every pair).
    • Transcripts get a timestamp about every 30 seconds instead of every 5 minutes.
    • Lecture pages re-read their slides every time they opened (and the suggestions bar did so on every redraw), slowing large lectures and growing memory.
    • Opening Settings → AI could freeze Lectern behind a Keychain password prompt; the Claude key is now read once.
    • Recent pages and the sidebar could keep entries for pages that no longer exist.
    • The Back and Forward tooltips showed the wrong shortcuts.
    • Release checks no longer touch the installed app's settings, Keychain or log, and the test suite no longer writes into your Lectern data.

    Install: download Lectern.dmg, drag Lectern to Applications, then right-click it and choose Open the first time (the app isn't notarized).

  • v1.7.022 Nov 2026Notes

    More summary types (including same headings as the PDF), much better flashcard generation, Study Packs, auto-processing, phone review and more

    This is the "1.3.0" feature release, numbered 1.7.0 so it can update 1.6.0.

    Added

    • Quick summary types at the top of Summary Studio and in every file, page and folder's right-click menu (Summarise as…): Brief, Standard, Long/Detailed, Dot points, Same headings as the PDF, Slide by slide, Key points only, Exam revision (with practice questions after each section), Explain simply and Cheat sheet. "More types…" reaches every format. Pin and drag to reorder your favourites; your last type is remembered per subject.
    • Organise by: the source's own headings (PDF bookmarks, numbered headings like "2.3", heading styles or slide titles, each with its page or slide range, to the depth you choose, with tiny sections merged), by topic, in source order, slide by slide, by week or lecture, or by learning outcome. Review the detected headings first: skip, rename or merge them.
    • Any combination of type, format, length, organisation, level, focus and options works, with a plain-English description before you generate ("Brief dot points under each of the source's 12 headings, about 600 words").
    • Accurate lengths: summaries are checked against the target and condensed or expanded (from the cached facts) when more than 20% off. Word count and reading time are shown.
    • Content type (science/maths, programming, humanities, law/business, health/biology) is detected and changes how summaries and cards are written; you can change it.
    • Compare two summaries side by side with differences highlighted; every generated version stays in the page's summary history.
    • A new flashcard generator built from the checked facts on every slide: density from Light to Exhaustive (or a number), an estimate of cards and time, many card types (question/answer, definitions both ways, fill-in-the-blank, lists, processes, compare, scenarios, formulas, code, diagram labels as image occlusion, optional true/false) with a difficulty mix, quality rules (one idea, clear question, short answer, nothing given away), a faithfulness check against each card's source, and duplicate detection against your existing cards. A coverage report lists slides without cards with "Generate for These".
    • Every generated card keeps its page, slide number, a source excerpt (shown under "Source" on the back), an optional slide thumbnail, "Open Slide" and "Jump to Source", and automatic subject, week, topic and type tags.
    • Card review screen: grouped by slide with thumbnails, filters by type and topic, accept or reject per card or slide, inline editing, change a card's type, J/K/A/R/E and ⇧A, "Similar to an existing card" comparisons, or skip review for trusted batches.
    • Make cards from a page, a summary, a folder (via the Processing Queue), quiz mistakes, past-paper questions or active recall, and "Top Up" a page with cards only for slides that don't have any.
    • Study Packs: one click makes a summary, flashcards, a 10-question practice quiz and a one-page cheat sheet from the same cached facts, in the background (Quick, Standard and Exam prep presets, editable in Settings).
    • Auto-file new lectures: watch folders such as Downloads; slide files named with your unit codes are offered (or filed automatically) into the right subject and week, with optional Study Packs, custom name patterns, and an undo list in the Inbox.
    • Review on Phone: a QR code opens a mobile review page on your home Wi-Fi (tap to flip, Again/Hard/Good/Easy or swipe, add to Home Screen). Reviews sync straight back and are queued if the connection drops. A one-time key, local network only, off after 2 hours idle, with a menu bar indicator.
    • Anki: export any deck, page, folder or subject to .apkg (basic, reversed and cloze cards, tags, images and a Source field), and import .apkg packages and Anki text exports (review history optional).
    • Glossary hover: subject glossary terms in your notes show their definition on hover; ⌘-click opens where they were defined (can be turned off per page).
    • A suggestions bar on lecture pages (summarise, make cards, transcribe the recording, review due cards), recent actions in the command palette, Repeat Last Action (⇧⌘R), an AI Actions list with Undo for summaries and cards, and a quick AI settings button in the toolbar.

    Changed

    • Start Reviews is now ⌥⇧⌘R (⇧⌘R repeats your last AI action).
    • Lengths: Standard is about 18%; new Brief (5%) and Long (45%).

    Fixed

    • Making flashcards with a small local model could fail with "couldn't be read" on some lectures. The new generator writes cards from short, checked facts and never fails a whole lecture because of one bad reply.
    • Summaries could cite a fact to the wrong slide when a small model, re-reading missed slides, quoted something from a neighbouring slide. Those facts are now dropped instead of being relabelled.
    • (Found while benchmarking the new generator, before release.) Small local models sometimes copied the prompt's example cards. Those were rejected, but the facts then got no card at all. Each fact now falls back to a fill-in-the-blank card in its own words, and short, fact-packed readings get enough cards.
    • Title and section-divider slides no longer get a filler line in summaries just to "cover" them.

    Benchmarks (llama3.2 3B on this Mac)

    • Flashcards, old generator → new at Standard (coverage of must-know facts / quality / duplicates / correct slide citations): physics 50/100/8/58 → 58/100/0/84; reading 29/100/0/91 → 71/100/0/100; tables 42/100/0/83 → 67/100/0/94; scanned 89/100/0/57 → 67/100/0/100; 162-slide lecture 11/89/0/0 → 63/100/0/93. Thorough reaches 67–100% coverage.
    • Summaries (recall / faithfulness): physics 83/100 → 100/100, long lecture 100/95 → 100/95, others unchanged at 89–100/100.

Clean-up removes repeated headers and footers, slide numbers, copyright lines, "Questions?"/"Thank you"/agenda/blank slides and build-up duplicates, and joins "(cont.)" slides, with a "Cleaned: removed N…" note you can open.
    • New sources: .srt and .vtt captions, transcripts with timestamps, recordings matched to slides, EPUB books by chapter, images and handwriting by OCR, and web articles with their title, author and date.
    • Active recall (key terms blurred; the ones you didn't know become flashcards), blurting (timed, typed or dictated, then compared with your notes and scored) and Feynman mode (explain to a curious AI student, then get a clarity score). A Daily Review on Home: due cards, five quick questions and one blurt, with settings for length and mix.
    • Past papers: import PDFs or scans, questions and marks are found and linked to your lectures; practise one at a time or as a timed mock, marked against your lectures and any official solution. Likely exam topics, and a readiness score per subject with your five weakest topics and a one-click Planner plan.
    • Listen (⌥⌘L): pages read aloud with the best installed voice, play/pause, 0.75–2× speed, skip by section, current sentence highlighted, maths read naturally; export a page or a week's summaries as an M4A audio file with chapters.
    • Semantic search with an Ollama embedding model (background indexing, keyword and meaning results combined); "Ask about this subject" uses it with citations; smart folders (saved searches) and an interactive graph of linked pages in the sidebar.
    • Quick capture from anywhere (⌃⌥Space): note, paste, link or screenshot (its text is read with OCR), with subject and tags; Services → Send to Lectern; an Inbox with "Suggest where this belongs".
    • References: look up DOIs (Crossref) and ISBNs (Open Library); APA 7, Harvard (AGPS), MLA 9, Chicago and IEEE; copy references and in-text citations; lists per subject or assessment as text, rich text or BibTeX.
    • Reading mode (⌥⌘R): line width, line, letter and word spacing, a reading ruler and the dyslexia-friendly OpenDyslexic font (SIL Open Font License, included).
    • Templates gallery with Weekly Review, Lab Report Planner, Essay Planner and Group Meeting Notes, and "Save as Template…" for any page.
    • Daily backups (keeps 14, in a folder you choose) with restore of one page or everything; detection of sync conflict copies with a side-by-side merge.
    • Model routing per task (facts, writing, checking, flashcards, chat, embeddings) with an "Auto" choice that picks the best installed model and falls back to Claude within your monthly limit.

    Fixed

    • PDF text no longer loses letters or splits words on some slides.

Questions

Do I need to pay for AI?

No. You can use free local models with Ollama. If you prefer Claude, a lecture's notes usually cost a few cents with your own API key.

Where are my notes stored?

In a University folder you choose: subjects are folders and pages are Markdown files. Put it in iCloud Drive or OneDrive for automatic backup.

Can I use my existing Anki decks?

Yes. Lectern imports and exports .apkg files.
